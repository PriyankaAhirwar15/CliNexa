# American Heart Association & WHO Clinical Reference: Cardiovascular Wellness

Cardiovascular health refers to the optimal functioning of the heart, blood vessels, and systemic arterial circulation. 

## Key Risk Indicators and Prevention
The American Heart Association emphasizes "Life's Essential 8" as key lifestyle and biometric measures for cardiovascular health:
1. Healthy Diet: Prioritizing whole vegetables, fruits, legumes, lean proteins, and unsaturated fatty acids while avoiding ultra-processed foods, added sugars, and trans fats.
2. Regular Physical Activity: Achieving at least 150 minutes of moderate aerobic exercise or 75 minutes of vigorous exercise each week.
3. Nicotine Avoidance: Smoking cessation reduces acute endothelial injury and lowers long-term arterial plaque accumulation.
4. Restorative Sleep: 7 to 9 hours of uninterrupted nightly sleep promotes nighttime blood pressure dipping and hormonal balance.
5. Healthy Weight: Maintaining a Body Mass Index (BMI) within the healthy reference envelope (18.5 - 24.9 kg/m²) reduces cardiac workload.
6. Blood Lipid Control: Maintaining LDL (Low-Density Lipoprotein) cholesterol under 100 mg/dL and non-HDL cholesterol within recommended targets.
7. Blood Glucose Regulation: Fasting blood glucose below 100 mg/dL prevents diabetic macrovascular and microvascular microangiopathy.
8. Blood Pressure Regulation: Keeping systolic blood pressure below 120 mmHg and diastolic below 80 mmHg minimizes arterial wall stress.

## Warning Signs and Evaluation
Chest pressure, radiating pain to the jaw or left shoulder, shortness of breath on mild exertion, and palpitations are clinical signals requiring prompt medical assessment by an emergency healthcare professional.
